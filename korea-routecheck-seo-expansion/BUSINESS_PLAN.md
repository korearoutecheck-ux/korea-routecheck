# Korea RouteCheck: operating plan

## The business

Korea RouteCheck is a free, interactive travel utility for people planning their first trip to South Korea. Its initial product builds realistic Seoul days by grouping nearby neighborhoods, limiting daily load, adding weather alternatives, and estimating a working budget.

The traveler does not buy a package from the owner. Revenue comes from optional booking links to established travel merchants.

## Why this product was selected

- It avoids client acquisition, custom orders, meetings, and revisions.
- It can launch with no paid API or hosting subscription.
- It uses a credible advantage: eight years of on-the-ground experience in Korea.
- Travel decisions have commercial intent. A visitor planning dates and activities is closer to purchase than a general entertainment audience.
- The product can expand from Seoul to Busan, Gyeongju, Jeju, airport transfers, day trips, and seasonal routes without changing the business model.
- The same software can produce search landing pages and shareable itinerary outputs.

## Monetization

### Primary: tours and activities

Viator states that its affiliate program is free, has no traffic minimum, uses a 30-day attribution window, and pays 8% on completed bookings. Viator handles payment and customer service after the click.

### Secondary: eSIM referrals

Airalo states that its standard affiliate rate is 10% of final sale value. It pays after the applicable threshold and review requirements are met.

### Later: additional travel partners and advertising

Additional programs should be added only after the site has traffic and conversion evidence. Display advertising is a later supplement, not the initial revenue engine.

## Illustrative revenue model, not a forecast

Assume an average completed activity booking value of $180 and an 8% commission:

- Commission per booking: $14.40
- 10 bookings: $144
- 35 bookings: $504
- 70 bookings: $1,008

At a hypothetical 1% visitor-to-completed-booking rate, 70 bookings would require approximately 7,000 qualified visits. Actual order values and conversion rates could be substantially lower or higher. Refunds, cancellations, attribution loss, seasonality, and affiliate-program changes all affect revenue.

## Time horizon

- Functional MVP: complete.
- Public deployment: possible after accounts, links, email, and domain placeholders are configured.
- First commission target: 2–6 weeks after launch, not guaranteed.
- Useful traffic base: normally a multi-month project.
- Sustainability threshold: enough search traffic that new visitors arrive without daily social posting.

This is not an emergency-cash guarantee. Its advantage is that each improvement becomes a durable asset instead of another hour sold.

## 60-day growth system

### Weeks 1–2: establish the product

- Deploy the MVP.
- Activate affiliate links.
- Publish four genuinely useful pages: 2-day, 3-day, 5-day, and 7-day Seoul plans.
- Submit the sitemap to search engines.
- Share the planner only in contexts where it directly answers a travel-planning question.

### Weeks 3–4: cover high-intent decisions

- Add pages for first-night planning, rainy days, family pacing, solo travel, and Seoul neighborhood selection.
- Add airport arrival logic.
- Review aggregate behavior and outbound-link clicks.

### Weeks 5–8: expand the route graph

- Add Busan and Gyeongju.
- Add Seoul day trips only where the booking link is relevant.
- Update weak pages based on search impressions and actual user behavior.
- Remove recommendations that receive clicks but produce poor outcomes or complaints.

## Automated operating loop

1. Check search demand and travel changes weekly.
2. Produce a proposed factual update or new high-intent page.
3. Verify travel claims against current authoritative sources.
4. Commit the update to the code repository.
5. Deploy automatically after approval.
6. Review privacy-preserving traffic and affiliate conversion data.
7. Prioritize the next change based on visitor usefulness and revenue per qualified visit.

## Owner involvement

The owner must retain control over:

- Affiliate applications, identity verification, tax forms, and payment accounts.
- Final approval of factual travel recommendations.
- Public business contact information.
- Any legally required disclosures or compliance decisions.
- Exceptional partner or visitor communications.

With the operating integrations connected, routine content planning, code changes, quality checks, deployment preparation, and performance summaries can be largely automated.

## Required integrations

### GitHub — required

Purpose: source control, change history, issue tracking, and free initial hosting through GitHub Pages.

### Cloudflare — optional after validation

Purpose: optional later migration for deployment controls, caching, and privacy-oriented traffic analytics. It is not required for the initial GitHub Pages launch.

### Gmail — required for low-touch operation

Purpose: affiliate-program approvals, operational alerts, partner messages, and a monitored public contact address. Sending or replying to sensitive messages should remain approval-gated.

No Airtable, paid AI API, paid domain, design subscription, or advertising account is required for the MVP.

## Principal risks

- Traffic may take months and may never reach a profitable level.
- Travel information becomes outdated and requires verification.
- Affiliate programs can change terms, attribution, rates, or eligibility.
- Search engines may not rank the site.
- Thin, repetitive, or inaccurate generated pages would damage trust and search performance.
- Revenue is seasonal and dependent on completed bookings.

## Go/no-go milestones

- Day 14: the site is indexed and initial pages are live.
- Day 30: at least 200 qualified visitors or clear search-impression growth.
- Day 60: at least 1,000 cumulative qualified visitors, measurable outbound booking clicks, or the first commission.
- If none occur, do not spend money. Reposition the tool or end the test.
